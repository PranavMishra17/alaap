# 14 · gramophone

**Lens.** The artefacts of the recorded voice as visual language only: a shellac disc on a
turntable, a paper label, typed catalogue cards, a sleeve, an archive's gaps register. No history
is asserted; no date, maker or format is named.

**Palette.** Card stock `#DDE0D7` (ground), shellac `#171211` (disc, ink, gaps register), bottle
green `#1F4A3C` (label, controls), old gold `#C9A24E` (label type, rim, focus), index-card blue
`#7E9BB0` (rules), index-card red `#9A3B31` (the one red rule, caveats).

**Type.** Eczar (display, Latin and Devanagari: headings, label brand, character names). Gentium
Book Plus (body; italic for every clip's words). Courier Prime (catalogue fields, eyebrows,
controls, definition line).

**Signature mechanic.** The grooves are the real audio: each clip is fetched, decoded, and its
loudness envelope becomes groove density. "Put on" any record: the label rewrites
itself with the exact words and the minting sentence, the tonearm settles, the disc spins and the
arm tracks inward with playback. "Play the scene in order" queues the five lines. On phones a
fixed strip carries the state.

**Tried and removed.** A gloss sheen over the disc (the one glassy effect; matte reads truer). The
scroll-jump to the turntable on phones (the strip already says what is playing). The label was
re-cut so long lines clear the arc text.

**Foregrounded.** §1 on the label and hero, §8 as the catalogue, §4 as sleeve notes, §7 as "how a
voice is cut", §5 as cards with caveats attached, §6 as the gaps register, §11.
