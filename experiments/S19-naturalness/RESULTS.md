# S19 — both stages cost naturalness, and the metrics cannot see it

**Run:** 2026-09-06 · IndicVoices-R Hindi · 3 utterances × 3 versions · 11 blind pairs · one listener
**Question:** `S14b` found the baseline sounds processed. Is it the codec or the model?

---

## Why the question needed splitting

`S14b` failed its own control usefully: the listener called an **untouched** render
"processed". So the synthesis is audibly synthetic — but that is not actionable. It does
not say whether to change the codec, the LM, or neither.

Three versions of the same utterance, same speaker, same text:

| | |
|---|---|
| **A** real | the original IndicVoices-R recording |
| **B** roundtrip | that recording encoded and decoded by MioCodec with its own global embedding — **the codec alone, no LM** |
| **C** synthesis | the same text generated by Indic-Mio, decoded with the same speaker's embedding — **codec and model** |

`A vs B` isolates the codec. `B vs C` isolates the model on top. `A vs C` is the total.

## Result — the control passed and both stages are audible

| comparison | heard correctly | wrong | tie |
|---|---|---|---|
| **codec cost** (A vs B) | 2 | 0 | 1 |
| **model cost** (B vs C) | 2 | 0 | 1 |
| **total** (A vs C) | **3** | 0 | 0 |
| **control** (real vs real) | — | — | **2** |

**Controls 2/2 tie.** Given two real recordings, the listener called them equally real both
times — including one where they volunteered *"diff sentences both natural"*. Without that,
nothing else here would be readable.

**Pooled over the nine test pairs: 7 correct, 0 wrong, 2 ties.** Seven decisive judgements,
all in the same direction, is `p ≈ 0.008` under guessing. The synthesis is distinguishable
from real speech, and that is now measured rather than inferred from one offhand remark.

### Neither stage dominates

This is the finding that decides what to do. The codec costs something on its own, and the
model costs something on top, in roughly equal measure. **There is no single component to
swap.**

Replacing the codec would recover at most half the gap. Replacing the LM likewise. That
reframes the naturalness problem as a whole-pipeline one rather than a component choice —
and it is the opposite of what a "the vocoder sounds bad" intuition would have suggested.

*(Per-stage, n=3 with only 2 decisive judgements each: `p = 0.25` per stage. The **pooled**
claim is solid; **which stage is worse** is not resolved and this design cannot resolve it
without many more items.)*

## ⚠️ The metrics cannot see what the listener heard

Measured on the same three utterances:

| version | snr_db | hnr_db | f0_mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| A real | 38.2 | 6.14 | 212.6 |
| B codec roundtrip | **38.2** | **5.96** | **213.4** |
| C full synthesis | 50.5 | 5.27 | 208.1 |

**A and B are indistinguishable on every axis this project measures** — 0.0 dB of SNR,
0.18 dB of HNR, 0.8 Hz of pitch. A listener told them apart 2 times out of 3, with no
errors.

Whatever the codec degrades, **it is not on the five axes captions are written over, nor on
the recording-quality axis.** Those axes were chosen to describe *identity*, and they do
that; this is a reminder that they were never a naturalness metric and cannot be pressed
into service as one.

That also explains why nothing in `S5`–`S18` caught this. Every quality gate in the project
— drift, consistency, CER, uniqueness — passed on audio a listener finds audibly synthetic.
**They are all identity and intelligibility gates. There is no naturalness gate.**

## The confound, found by measurement before the listening

The codec **preserves the room noise exactly** (38.2 → 38.2); the LM generates clean audio
(50.5). So on any pair involving C, "which is real" could be answered by "which has room
tone", and the real-vs-real control cannot catch it because both its sides are noisy.

The instructions therefore told the listener explicitly to ignore background noise and
judge rhythm, stress, phrasing and pauses. **`A vs B` is free of the confound** — both
sides sit at 38.2 dB — and it is the comparison that carries the surprising result, so the
main finding does not depend on that instruction being followed.

## Not established

- **3 utterances, one listener, one language.** Enough to establish that a difference
  exists; nowhere near enough to size it, and not enough to rank the two stages against
  each other.
- **No MOS, no scale.** "Distinguishable from real" is not the same as "bad". A listener
  can reliably tell studio speech from a good recording of the same actor, and this design
  cannot say where on that spectrum the synthesis sits.
- **The A-vs-C and B-vs-C arms carry the noise confound** and rest on the listener having
  followed the instruction to ignore it.
- **Indic-Mio at 0.6B on a 6 GB card.** A larger model or a different codec variant might
  behave differently, and neither was tested.
- **Nothing was changed as a result.** No naturalness gate has been added, and this does
  not alter any identity result — those were measured against controls that still stand.

## Reproduce

```bash
envs/qwen3/Scripts/python.exe experiments/S19-naturalness/run_naturalness.py --phase codec
envs/qwen3/Scripts/python.exe experiments/S19-naturalness/run_naturalness.py --phase lm
envs/qwen3/Scripts/python.exe experiments/S19-naturalness/run_naturalness.py --phase decode
envs/qwen3/Scripts/python.exe experiments/S19-naturalness/run_naturalness.py --phase pairs
```

Four processes, because loading the codec and the LM together segfaulted with 2.7 GB of
15.7 GB system RAM free — nothing to do with the 6 GB GPU, which sat at 614 MiB. Each phase
keeps one model resident and caches its output; clips are cached too, so a re-run never
streams.
