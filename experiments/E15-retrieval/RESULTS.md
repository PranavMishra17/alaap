# E15 — the sentence encoder is the weak link in retrieval

**Run:** 2026-09-05 · 150 held-out speakers · 350 anchors · **CPU only, nothing rendered**
**Question:** should description→voice retrieval go through a sentence encoder at all?

---

## Why

E14b found that `f0_mean` alone predicts voice distance better than all five axes weighted equally — the description is not only too narrow, it is badly **weighted**. But the mapper never sees the axes. It retrieves by cosine similarity between **MiniLM sentence embeddings** of the captions, so the weighting it applies is whatever MiniLM assigns to the words *"low-pitched"* against *"rough"* against *"quick"*. Nothing chose that.

Since captions are *generated* from bins, and free text can be parsed back to bins (`captions.target_bins_from_text`), there is a direct alternative: retrieve in bin space, with weights measured from the corpus.

Weights are measured on the **fit half only**, as each axis's rank correlation between its own bin distance and voice distance, normalised to mean 1:

| axis | weight |
|---|---|
| **`f0_mean`** | **2.71** |
| **`vtl_cm`** | **1.44** |
| `spectral_tilt` | 0.66 |
| `hnr_db` | 0.56 |
| `f0_cv` | 0.33 |
| `speaking_rate` | 0.31 |

Pitch and vocal-tract length carry most of the identity information; rate and expressiveness carry very little. That is consistent with E14b and with the anatomy — F0 is the larynx, VTL is the tube, and both are physical size. Rate and expressiveness are *behaviour*, which a speaker can change at will and which therefore should not identify them.

## Result

| method | **cos to true voice** | **rank of true (chance = 175)** | transport ρ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **text (current)** | 0.0958 | 129.7 | 0.280 |
| bin, equal weights | 0.1544 | 101.5 | 0.459 |
| **bin, measured weights** | **0.1587** | **96.3** | 0.484 |

*(`transport ρ` is measured in bin space and therefore flatters the bin methods. `cos to true voice` and the rank are the honest columns — they score the voice.)*

**The current text path is barely better than chance.** Retrieving a held-out speaker's caption ranks their true nearest anchor at **129.7 out of 350**, where random guessing gives 175. Bin retrieval reaches **96.3**, and cos-to-true-voice improves by **+66%** (0.0958 → 0.1587).

**Most of the gain is the representation, not the weighting.** Equal-weight bins already deliver 0.1544; measured weights add 0.0043 more. So the finding is not primarily "weight the axes better" — it is **"stop passing structured data through a sentence encoder."** The captions are generated from six numbers; embedding them into 384 dimensions of general-purpose sentence semantics and taking cosines discards most of that structure.

## What to change, and the caveat that limits it

**Retrieve on bins, not on sentence embeddings** — but this experiment measures the *upper bound* of that change, and the gap to real use matters:

> Here, every test caption was **generated by `caption_from_bins`**, so its bins are exactly recoverable by construction. A real user types *"a gravelly old sailor, world-weary"*. `target_bins_from_text` will recover some axes from that and none of the rest, and the ungrounded part ("world-weary") carries no bins at all.

So the shipping design is a **hybrid**, not a replacement: parse what the text yields, retrieve on those axes with these weights, and fall back to the text encoder for whatever is left. The measured gain applies only to the parsed portion.

Two other limits: 350 anchors here against 2,500 in the real mapper — retrieval quality generally improves with anchor density, and the methods may not scale identically. And nothing was rendered, so this is retrieval quality in embedding space, not audio.

## Not established

- One corpus, English, one backend, `top_k=4`, one SLERP blending scheme.
- The weights are measured on 350 speakers and are not validated on a second corpus.
- No rendering: no drift, consistency, or adherence, and no listening.
- The hybrid design above is **proposed, not tested**. The number that matters for it — how much of a real user's description `target_bins_from_text` recovers — has not been measured.
